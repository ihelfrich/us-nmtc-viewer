\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scope of the results}

The median and two margin specifications produce small within-CDE rural
estimates that are not statistically distinguishable from zero. The mean
point estimate is smaller than the raw gap, but its confidence interval is
wide. In the descriptive decomposition, most explained movement is
associated with observed CDE composition. This does not make rural and urban
credit markets equivalent, establish the absence of a within-CDE component,
or identify why CDEs differ.

The mechanism behind composition remains open. Intermediary selection is a
natural reading of the between-CDE component. Rural market conditions could
also lead low-leverage intermediaries to specialize in rural deployment,
placing part of that component on the market-structure side of the ledger.
Separating these accounts requires variation in CDE--market assignment that
the public release lacks. The estimates also average over the fixed-effects
distribution; they do not set a ceiling on what any particular CDE could
mobilize in rural tracts.

\subsection{Policy reading}

Under the existing program design, the estimates motivate evaluating
\emph{intermediary-allocation} levers alongside project- and market-level
interventions. They do not show that a marginal rural deal is unconstrained
by market structure or that reallocating credits would change mobilization.
Those policy effects require variation that the descriptive design does not
provide.

The 20\% mandate operates on cumulative deployment share, where the data
show no bunching. Deployment data alone cannot establish whether the target
binds through allocation agreements or whether a different policy margin
would improve rural mobilization.

\subsection{Research program}

The next step is a multi-program U.S. comparison covering NMTC, LIHTC, and
Opportunity Zones, followed by an international extension to
development-bank project finance. The same mobilization framework applies
in each setting. More immediately, the LIC-eligibility regression
discontinuity requires the ACS tract merge.
